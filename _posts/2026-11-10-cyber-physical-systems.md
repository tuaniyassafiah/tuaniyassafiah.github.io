## 1. Introduction

Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) are an important technology related to the Internet of Things (IoT) and smart manufacturing. CPS combines physical equipment with computer systems, communication networks, sensors and control technologies. This integration allows machines and digital systems to interact and exchange information.

According to the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), CPS involves interconnected physical and computational components that work together to perform specific functions. In manufacturing, CPS helps connect production equipment with digital technologies to support monitoring, automation and decision-making.

## 2. Main Components of Cyber-Physical Systems

A CPS environment generally consists of several important components:

**2.1 Physical Components**

Physical components refer to machines, equipment, robots and other devices that perform activities in the real world. In manufacturing, examples include conveyor systems, robotic arms and production machines.

**2.2 Sensors**

Sensors collect information from physical equipment and the surrounding environment. They can measure parameters such as temperature, pressure, vibration and machine operating conditions.

**2.3 Computational Systems**

Computational systems process the information collected by sensors. They use software, embedded computers or controllers to analyse data and determine appropriate actions.

**2.4 Communication Networks**

Communication networks allow different components to exchange information. They connect machines, sensors, controllers and other digital systems within a manufacturing environment.

**2.5 Actuators**

Actuators perform physical actions based on instructions received from control systems. Examples include motors, valves and robotic mechanisms.

## 3. How Cyber-Physical Systems Work

CPS operates through continuous interaction between physical equipment and digital systems.

The general process involves the following steps:

1. Sensors collect information from physical machines or equipment.
2. The collected information is transmitted to a computational system.
3. The computational system processes and analyses the information.
4. A control decision is generated based on the analysed data.
5. Actuators receive instructions and perform the required physical action.
6. Sensors continue monitoring the process and provide updated information.

This process creates a feedback loop between the physical and digital environments. As a result, the system can monitor conditions and respond to changes during operation.

## 4. Applications of CPS in Manufacturing

CPS has several applications in modern manufacturing industries.

**4.1 Automated Production**

CPS connects machines, sensors and control systems to support automated production processes. This allows equipment to operate in a coordinated manner with less manual intervention.

**4.2 Machine Condition Monitoring**

Sensors monitor machine conditions such as temperature and vibration. The collected information helps operators identify unusual operating conditions and potential equipment problems.

**4.3 Predictive Maintenance**

CPS can support predictive maintenance by analysing machine operating data. This helps manufacturers identify possible equipment failures before they cause major production interruptions.

**4.4 Quality Control**

CPS can support product quality monitoring by collecting production data and identifying process variations. This helps manufacturers detect potential quality problems during production.

**4.5 Robotic Systems**

Robotic equipment can interact with sensors and computer-based control systems to perform activities such as material handling, assembly and product inspection.

## 5. Benefits of CPS in Manufacturing

The implementation of CPS can provide several benefits:

- **Improved productivity:** Connected equipment can support more coordinated production activities.
- **Real-time monitoring:** Operators can receive updated information about machine and production conditions.
- **Reduced downtime:** Monitoring and predictive maintenance can help identify equipment problems earlier.
- **Better decision-making:** Data analysis provides useful information for production planning and control.
- **Improved process efficiency:** Integration between physical and digital systems supports more effective manufacturing operations.

## 6. Challenges of Implementing CPS

Although CPS offers various benefits, manufacturers may face several challenges during implementation.

**6.1 Cybersecurity Risks**

Connected machines and communication networks may be exposed to cybersecurity threats. Therefore, manufacturers need suitable security measures to protect their systems and production data.

**6.2 High Implementation Costs**

The implementation of CPS may require investment in sensors, communication infrastructure, software and specialised equipment.

**6.3 System Integration**

Different machines and technologies may use different communication standards and systems. This can make integration more complicated.

**6.4 Technical Skills**

Workers and engineers may require additional technical knowledge to operate, maintain and manage CPS-based manufacturing systems.

## 7. Conclusion

Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) integrate physical equipment with computational systems and communication technologies. Through sensors, data processing and actuators, CPS allows manufacturing equipment to interact with digital systems and respond to changing operating conditions.

In modern manufacturing, CPS supports automated production, machine monitoring, predictive maintenance and quality control. Despite challenges such as cybersecurity risks, implementation costs and system integration, CPS plays an important role in the development of smart manufacturing and Industry 4.0.
